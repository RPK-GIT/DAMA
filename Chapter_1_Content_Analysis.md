## 4. Important Definitions (to preserve EXACTLY)

1. **Data Management** (p. 45): "Data Management is the development, execution, and supervision of plans, policies, programs, and practices that deliver, control, protect, and enhance the value of data and information assets throughout their lifecycles."
2. **Data Management Professional** (p. 45): "A Data Management Professional is any person who works in any facet of data management (from technical management of data throughout its lifecycle to ensuring that data is properly utilized and leveraged) to meet strategic organizational goals."
3. **Asset** (p. 49): "An asset is an economic resource, that can be owned or controlled, and that holds or produces value. Assets can be converted to money."
4. **Value** (p. 55): "Value is the difference between the cost of a thing and the benefit derived from that thing."
5. **Strategy / Strategic plan** (p. 66): "A strategy is a set of choices and decisions that together chart a high-level course of action to achieve high-level goals." / "A strategic plan is a high-level course of action to achieve high-level goals."
6. **Data lineage** (p. 61): "a pathway along which it moves from its point of origin to its point of usage, sometimes called the data chain."
7. **Data ROT** (p. 63): "Data that is Redundant, Obsolete, Trivial (Aiken, 2014)."
8. The **11 Knowledge Area definitions** (pp. 89–90): Data Governance, Data Architecture, Data Modeling and Design, Data Storage and Operations, Data Security, Data Integration and Interoperability, Document and Content Management, Reference and Master Data, Data Warehousing and Business Intelligence, Metadata, Data Quality — each preserved verbatim.

## 5. Key Concepts by Section

- **1 Introduction:** data as vital enterprise asset; deriving value requires intention, planning, coordination, commitment, management, leadership; data management requires technical AND non-technical (business) skills; responsibility shared between business and IT; data called 'currency', 'life blood', 'new oil'; DMBOK2 produced by DAMA International (2nd edition; 1st edition 2009).
- **1.1 Business Drivers:** information/knowledge = key to competitive advantage; reliable high-quality data → better decisions; failure to manage data is like failure to manage capital (waste, lost opportunity); primary driver = enable organizations to get value from data assets.
- **1.2 Goals:** six goals: understand/support information needs; capture, store, protect, ensure integrity; ensure quality; ensure privacy & confidentiality; prevent unauthorized/inappropriate access, manipulation, use; ensure effective use to add value.
- **2.1 Data:** data represents facts; also understood as information stored in digital form (but not limited to digitized data); data is a means of representation — "stands for things other than itself" (Chisholm, 2010); data is both an interpretation of the objects it represents and an object that must be interpreted (Sebastian-Coleman, 2013); context = data's representational system; conventions documented as Metadata; multiple representations of same concept → need for Data Architecture, modeling, governance, stewardship, Metadata & Data Quality management; industry-level data standards.
- **2.2 Data and Information:** data called "raw material of information", information called "data in context"; DIKW pyramid (data → information → knowledge → wisdom) and its 3 challenges: (a) data does not simply exist, it has to be created; (b) linear sequence fails to recognize it takes knowledge to create data; (c) data and information are intertwined, not separate. Sales report example (information vs data). Central tenet: BOTH data and information need to be managed; used interchangeably in DMBOK.
- **2.3 Data as Organizational Asset:** asset definition; goodwill analogy (P&L); monetization of data increasingly common; data-driven organizations: event triggers + analytics instead of gut feelings; partnership of business leadership + technical expertise; digital disruption is the norm; co-create information solutions.
- **2.4 Principles (12, Figure 1 grouping):** Data is valuable: (1) Data is an asset with unique properties; (2) The value of data can and should be expressed in economic terms. DM requirements are business requirements: (3) Managing data means managing the quality of data; (4) It takes Metadata to manage data; (5) It takes planning to manage data; (6) Data management requirements must drive Information Technology decisions. DM depends on diverse skills: (7) Data management is cross-functional; (8) Data management requires an enterprise perspective; (9) Data management must account for a range of perspectives. DM is lifecycle management: (10) Data management is lifecycle management; (11) Different types of data have different lifecycle characteristics; (12) Managing data includes managing the risks associated with data. Plus: Effective data management requires leadership commitment.
- **2.5 Challenges (13):** each challenge maps back to principles (2.5.1–2.5.13, detailed below).
- **2.5.1:** data not tangible; durable (doesn't wear out but value changes with age); easy to copy/transport; not easy to reproduce if lost; not consumed when used; can be stolen without being gone; multi-purpose; simultaneous multi-user use; data begets more data; challenges: monetary value, ownership, inventorying, misuse protection, redundancy risk, quality standards; data is a meta-asset describing other assets.
- **2.5.2 Valuation:** cost/benefit categories: cost of obtaining & storing; cost of replacing if lost; impact if missing; cost of risk mitigation & potential risk cost; cost of improving data; benefits of higher quality data; what competitors would pay; what it could be sold for; expected revenue from innovative uses. Value is contextual and temporal. Valuation as change management.
- **2.5.3 Quality:** "garbage in, garbage out"; trust once lost is hard to regain; 10–30% of revenue spent handling DQ issues; IBM: cost of poor quality data in US 2016 = $3.1 Trillion; costs of poor quality data (9 items: scrap & rework … reputational costs); benefits of high quality data (6 items: improved customer experience … competitive advantage).
- **2.5.4 Planning:** data as a product; systems thinking: data connects business processes; relationship between processes & technology; system design/architecture & data; data used to advance strategy; balance long- and short-term goals.
- **2.5.5 Metadata:** Metadata describes what data an org has, what it represents, classification, origin, movement, evolution, who can use it, quality; Metadata is itself data and must be managed; Metadata management as starting point for improvement.
- **2.5.6 Cross-functional:** skill range: design, technical, data analysis, analytic, language, strategic thinking; challenge = collaboration toward common goals.
- **2.5.7 Enterprise Perspective:** data is a 'horizontal' moving across verticals (sales, marketing, operations); data viewed as by-product of processes; disparate representations (customer, product, vendor); data governance helps decisions across verticals.
- **2.5.8 Other Perspectives:** internal + external (acquired) data; legal & compliance across national/industry lines; data creators forget later users; misuse risk.
- **2.5.9 Lifecycle (Figure 2):** Plan → Design & Enable → Create/Obtain → Store/Maintain → Use → Enhance (loop back to Plan); Store/Maintain → Dispose of (dashed). Lifecycle based on product lifecycle, NOT systems development lifecycle. Lineage/data chain. Implications: (1) Creation and usage are the most critical points; (2) Data Quality managed throughout lifecycle; (3) Metadata Quality managed through lifecycle; (4) Data Security managed throughout lifecycle; (5) focus on most critical data, minimize data ROT.
- **2.5.10 Types of Data:** classification by type (transactional, Reference, Master, Metadata; alternatively category, resource, event, detailed transaction data), by content (domains, subject areas), by format, by protection level, by how/where stored or accessed; tools focus on classification and control (Bryce, 2005); Master Data vs transactional data have different requirements.
- **2.5.11 Data and Risk:** low quality data = risk; misunderstanding/misuse risk; information gaps = difference between what we know and what we need to know = enterprise liabilities; regulations: Sarbanes-Oxley (financial transaction data controls), Solvency II (data lineage/quality for insurance risk models), data privacy regulations; data on the risk register; consumer expectations of protection and privacy.
- **2.5.12 Technology:** data management intertwined with IT but managing technology ≠ managing data; tension between new technology and reliable data; data requirements aligned with business strategy should drive technology decisions, not technological temptation.
- **2.5.13 Leadership:** Leader's Data Manifesto (2017): "organization's best opportunities for organic growth lie in data"; orgs confuse data and IT; CDO advocacy — business-driven not IT-driven; CDO leads initiatives AND cultural change.
- **2.6 Strategy:** strategy/strategic plan definitions; data strategy comes from understanding data needs in business strategy; Data Management program strategy; typically owned by CDO, enacted through data governance team, supported by Data Governance Council; 10 strategy components (vision, business case, guiding principles, mission & goals, success measures, SMART 12–24 month objectives, roles & organizations, program components, prioritized work program, draft roadmap); 3 deliverables: Data Management Charter, Data Management Scope Statement (usually 3-year horizon), Data Management Implementation Roadmap.
- **3 Frameworks:** why frameworks: see relationships between components, clarify strategy, develop roadmaps, organize teams, align functions; application depends on industry, range of data, culture, maturity level, strategy, vision, specific challenges; the 5 frameworks positioned: SAM & AIM = high-level relationships; DAMA DMBOK Framework = Knowledge Areas; last two rearrange the Wheel to show relationships.
- **3.1 SAM (Henderson & Venkatraman):** center = relationship between data and information; information ↔ business strategy & operational use; data ↔ IT & processes; 4 domains of strategic choice: Business Strategy, IT Strategy, Organizational Infrastructure & Processes, IT Infrastructure & Processes; strategic fit + functional integration.
- **3.2 AIM (Abcouwer, Maes, Truijens, 1997):** the 9-cell; middle layer = structure and tactics (planning, architecture); information communication (information governance and data quality pillar).
- **3.3 DAMA-DMBOK Framework:** 3 visuals — DAMA Wheel (Fig 5): Data Governance at center + 10 Knowledge Areas around; Environmental Factors hexagon (Fig 6): Goals & Principles at center, inner ring (Roles & Responsibilities, Activities, Deliverables, Tools, Techniques, Organization & Culture), outer band (People, Process, Technology); Context Diagram (Fig 7): based on SIPOC; activities at center classified P-D-O-C (Plan, Develop, Operate, Control); 12 component pieces: Definition, Goals, Activities, Inputs, Deliverables, Roles & Responsibilities, Suppliers, Consumers, Participants, Tools, Techniques, Metrics.
- **3.4 Aiken Pyramid (Fig 8):** Phase 1: application with database capabilities (modeling/design, storage, security; work on integration & interoperability); Phase 2: data quality challenges → reliable Metadata + consistent Data Architecture; Phase 3: disciplined DQ/Metadata/architecture practice requires Data Governance → enables strategic initiatives (Document & Content Mgmt, Reference Data Mgmt, MDM, Data Warehousing, BI) enabling golden-pyramid advanced practices; Phase 4: leverage well-managed data, advance analytic capabilities. Two drivers: building on a foundation vs. pieces put in place in arbitrary order.
- **3.5 Framework Evolved:** Geuens dependencies (Fig 9): BI/analytics depend on Master Data & data warehouse solutions → which depend on feeding systems and applications → which depend on Data Quality, data design, interoperability; data governance (incl. Metadata Mgmt, security, architecture, Reference Data Mgmt) is the foundation. Fig 10 (DAMA Function Framework): purpose = value from data assets; lifecycle functions in the center (plan & design; enable & maintain; use & enhance); emerging functions (Big Data storage, Data Science); data monetization; foundational activities (data risk management, Metadata, DQ management) span lifecycle; oversight = data governance (strategy, principles, policies, stewardship, culture change). Fig 11 (Wheel Evolved): core activities (Metadata Mgmt, DQ Mgmt, data structure definition/architecture) at center; lifecycle management from planning perspective (risk management, modeling, data design, Reference Data Mgmt) and enablement perspective (MDM, data technology development, data integration & interoperability, data warehousing, data storage and operations); usages (Master data usage, Document & content mgmt, BI, Data Science, predictive analytics, data visualization; data monetization); governance provides oversight/containment (classification, valuation).
- **4 DAMA and the DMBOK:** managing data recognized as central to success since at least the 1980s; DMBOK supports DAMA's mission 3 ways (functional framework; common vocabulary; fundamental reference guide for CDMP and other certification exams); structured around 11 Knowledge Areas (Chapters 3–13); common chapter structure: 1. Introduction (Business Drivers, Goals and Principles, Essential Concepts), 2. Activities, 3. Tools, 4. Techniques, 5. Implementation Guidelines, 6. Relation to Data Governance, 7. Metrics; 11 KA definitions verbatim; other chapters: Data Handling Ethics (2), Big Data and Data Science (14), DM Maturity Assessment (15), DM Organization and Role Expectations (16), DM and Organizational Change Management (17).
- **5 Works Cited / Recommended:** bibliography (Aiken, Chisholm, Redman, Sebastian-Coleman, Henderson & Venkatraman, Abcouwer/Maes/Truijens, English, McGilvray, etc.) plus endnotes (dictionary/ASQ/ISO 11179 definitions of data; Redman properties of data; Wannacry example of data valuation).
